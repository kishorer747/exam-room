# Examiner bank 1: Contribution margin

Lesson: `course/lessons/01-contribution-margin.md`

> **Examiner's eyes only.** This bank belongs in the examiner's working folder. Keeping it there is a convention for an honest self-study exam, not a security boundary.
>
> **Every figure is illustrative and invented for teaching.** Marrow Hill Soap and any other business named here are fictional.

## How to mark

- Ask the question exactly as written. Each one stands alone and allows a calculator.
- Mark each answer **correct** or **incorrect**. There is no half mark. Apply "Correct only if" literally, and use "Incorrect if" for the wrong answers people usually give.
- Figures must fall inside the stated tolerance. Different wording is fine if the same idea is clearly there.
- A sound method with a wrong final figure is incorrect. Record the weak spot as `arithmetic-slip`, not the question's own label, and let the person overrule it if they spot the slip.
- If a required part is missing, you may ask once: "Is there anything to add?" Do not hint at what is missing. Mark what you then have.
- Never quote the question's answer, these notes or a worked figure in the room. Post only the weak spot label and its lesson section.

## CM-Q1

**Question.** Illustrative figures. Marrow Hill Soap, a fictional business, sells a monthly box for $40. For each box it pays $8 for ingredients, $2 for packaging, $6 for shipping and a payment fee of 4% of the price. Each month it also pays $3,000 workshop rent and a $5,000 founder salary. What are the contribution margin per box and the contribution margin ratio?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: variable cost 8 + 2 + 6 + 1.60 = $17.60. CM = **$22.40**. Ratio = 22.40 / 40 = **56%**.
- Correct only if: both $22.40 and 56% are given (tolerance: exact, 56.0% accepted).
- Incorrect if: $24.00 and 60% (left out the fee); any answer that spreads rent or salary across boxes; $23.36 (took 4% of the $16 of other costs, not of the price).

**Weak spot revealed.** `variable-cost-sorting`. Lesson section: "What one more box earns". Leaves out percentage costs or brings fixed costs into CM.

## CM-Q2

**Question.** Illustrative figures. For a fictional subscription box business, say whether each cost belongs in contribution margin, and give a reason for each: (a) ads aimed at winning new customers; (b) packing staff paid $1 per box packed; (c) a salaried support agent who can handle 2,000 customers, when the business has 900; (d) refunds averaging 2% of orders.

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: (a) out, it is acquisition cost and is counted in CAC; (b) in, it moves with each box; (c) out, it is fixed until capacity is reached, then steps up; (d) in, it moves with orders.
- Correct only if: all four are classified as above, and the reasons for (a) and (c) are given. For (c), the reason must rest on spare capacity or the cost not moving with one more box.
- Incorrect if: (c) is called variable because "support scales with customers" with no mention of capacity; (a) is put in CM; any item is wrong, even if the other three are right.

**Weak spot revealed.** `one-more-box-test`. Lesson section: "What counts as variable". Sorts costs by their name, not by asking whether one more sale moves them.

## CM-Q3

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box business sells at $40 with a contribution margin of $22.40 per box. Fixed costs are $11,200 a month. How many boxes a month does it need to break even? If it then adds a hire costing $2,800 a month, how many more boxes does it need?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: 11,200 / 22.40 = **500 boxes**. 2,800 / 22.40 = **125 more** (625 in total).
- Correct only if: both 500 and 125 (or 625 in total, clearly stated) are given. Tolerance: exact.
- Incorrect if: 280 and 70 (divided by price); 500 with any other second figure.

**Weak spot revealed.** `break-even-divides-by-cm`. Lesson section: "Break-even". Uses price, not contribution, to cover fixed costs.

## CM-Q4

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box sells at $40. Costs that do not depend on price are $16 a box, and the payment fee is 4% of whatever price is charged. The business considers 25% off. What is the new contribution per box, by what percentage does contribution per box fall, and how many more boxes (as a percentage) does it need to earn the same total contribution?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: normal CM = 40 - 16 - 1.60 = $22.40. Discounted price $30, fee $1.20, CM = **$12.80**. Fall = 9.60 / 22.40 = **42.9%**. Volume = 22.40 / 12.80 = 1.75, so **75% more**.
- Correct only if: all three figures are given. Tolerance: $12.80 exact, fall 42.5% to 43%, volume exactly 75%.
- Incorrect if: $12.40 and about 81% (kept the fee at $1.60); 25% fall; 33% more boxes (reversed the price cut alone and ignored costs).

**Weak spot revealed.** `discount-hits-contribution`. Lesson section: "Discounts cost more than they look". Treats a price cut as if it removed the same percentage of profit.

## CM-Q5

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box sells at $40. The business books only ingredients and packaging, $8 in total, as cost of goods, and so reports 80% gross margin. It also pays $6 shipping and a 4% payment fee per box. The founder says: "With 80% margin, another $10,000 of sales gives us $8,000 towards fixed costs." Is the founder right? Give the figure you would use.

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: no. CM = 40 - 8 - 6 - 1.60 = $24.40, a ratio of 61%. Another $10,000 of sales is 250 boxes and adds **$6,100**.
- Correct only if: says the founder is wrong, gives $6,100 (or 61% applied to the $10,000), and names shipping and the fee as the costs left out. Tolerance: exact.
- Incorrect if: $6,500 or 65% (left out the fee); $7,600 or 76% (left out shipping); says "right, because gross margin is the standard measure".

**Weak spot revealed.** `gross-margin-is-not-cm`. Lesson section: "Contribution margin is not gross margin". Trusts a reported margin without checking what it leaves out.

## CM-Q6

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional business sells a small box at $20 with $9 contribution, and a large box at $50 with $15 contribution. Scenario A: a $1,000 ad budget brings either 100 small-box sales or 50 large-box sales. Scenario B: the workshop can serve only 100 customers a month, and it can fill those places with either box. Which box should it push in each scenario? Show the contribution for each option, and state the principle.

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: A: small gives 100 x 9 = $900, large gives 50 x 15 = $750, so **small**. B: small gives $900, large gives 100 x 15 = $1,500, so **large**. Principle: compare contribution per unit of whatever is scarce (ad budget in A, customer places in B).
- Correct only if: both choices are right, the four contribution totals are shown ($900 and $750, $900 and $1,500), and the principle names the limiting resource.
- Incorrect if: the same box is chosen in both; the choice rests only on "higher percentage" or "higher dollars" with no mention of the constraint.

**Weak spot revealed.** `margin-per-scarce-resource`. Lesson section: "Ratio or dollars: ask what is scarce". Ranks products by one margin figure without asking what limits the business.

## CM-Q7

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box sells at $40, with a variable cost of $17.60 a box (ingredients $8, packaging $2, shipping $6, payment fee $1.60). The accountant adds $12.40 of allocated fixed costs for a "full cost" of $30. A hotel offers $20 a box for a one-off order of 300 boxes. It will collect them and pay by invoice, so there is no shipping and no payment fee. The accountant says to refuse because it loses $10 a box. What do you advise, what is the effect on monthly profit, and what would you check first?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: accept, subject to checks. Variable cost is $10 (ingredients and packaging), so each box adds $10 and the order adds **$3,000**. Fixed costs do not change. Check spare capacity, and that regular subscribers will not expect the same price or stop buying at full price.
- Correct only if: advises accepting (or accepting on stated conditions), gives +$3,000, and names at least one of the two checks. Tolerance: exact.
- Incorrect if: refuses on the $10 "loss"; $720 (kept shipping and the fee: 20 - 17.60 = $2.40 a box); accepts with no check named.

**Weak spot revealed.** `full-cost-trap`. Lesson section: "The full-cost trap". Treats allocated fixed cost as if the decision could save it.
