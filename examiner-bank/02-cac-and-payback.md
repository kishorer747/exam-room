# Examiner bank 2: CAC and payback

Lesson: `course/lessons/02-cac-and-payback.md`

> **Examiner's eyes only.** This bank belongs in the examiner's working folder. Keeping it there is a convention for an honest self-study exam, not a security boundary.
>
> **Every figure is illustrative and invented for teaching.** Marrow Hill Soap and any other business named here are fictional.

## How to mark

- Ask the question exactly as written. Each one stands alone and allows a calculator.
- Mark each answer **correct** or **incorrect**. There is no half mark. Apply "Correct only if" literally, and use "Incorrect if" for the wrong answers people usually give.
- Figures must fall inside the stated tolerance. Different wording is fine if the same idea is clearly there.
- A sound method with a wrong final figure is incorrect. Record the weak spot as `arithmetic-slip`, not the question's own label, and let the person overrule it if they spot the slip.
- If a required part is missing, you may ask once: "Is there anything to add?" Do not hint at what is missing. Mark what you then have.
- Never quote the question's answer, these notes or a worked figure in the room. Post only the weak spot label and its lesson section.

## CAC-Q1

**Question.** Illustrative figures. In one month, Marrow Hill Soap, a fictional business, spends $9,000 on ads, $1,500 on an agency fee, and half of a marketer's $5,000 salary on winning customers. It gains 400 new customers: 250 credited to paid channels and 150 from word of mouth. What are the fully loaded blended CAC and the fully loaded paid CAC? Which would you use to judge the paid channels, and why?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: total = 9,000 + 1,500 + 2,500 = $13,000. Blended = 13,000 / 400 = **$32.50**. Paid = 13,000 / 250 = **$52.00**. Use paid, because word-of-mouth customers were not won by that spend and flatter the blended figure.
- Correct only if: both figures and the choice of paid are given, with that reason. An answer that prefers marginal or incremental CAC for this job is also fine, provided both figures are right and paid is preferred to blended. Tolerance: exact.
- Incorrect if: $22.50 or $36 (ads only); $26.25 or $42 (left out the salary); $38.75 or $62 (counted the whole salary); chooses blended.

**Weak spot revealed.** `loaded-cac-right-denominator`. Lesson section: "Count all of it, and divide by the right customers". Leaves cost out or spreads it over customers it did not win.

## CAC-Q2

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box sells at $40 a month with a contribution margin of $22.40. CAC is $52. The founder says: "52 divided by 40, so we get our money back in under two months." Ignoring churn, what is the right payback, and in which monthly box is the CAC recovered?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: 52 / 22.40 = 2.32 months, so it is recovered in the **3rd box**. After two boxes only $44.80 has come back, and after three $67.20. The founder used revenue, which also has to pay for the box.
- Correct only if: states about 2.3 months (2.3 to 2.33 accepted) and the 3rd box, and says contribution, not revenue, is what repays CAC.
- Incorrect if: 1.3 months or 2nd box; 2.32 with no mention of why revenue is wrong; states the 2nd box because 2.32 rounds down.

**Weak spot revealed.** `payback-on-contribution`. Lesson section: "Payback uses contribution, not revenue". Counts revenue as money returned.

## CAC-Q3

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box earns $22.40 contribution a month. CAC is $60. Every customer pays for box 1, and then a fixed percentage stay for each later box. In which box is the CAC recovered per customer won if monthly retention is 80%? What if monthly retention is 60%?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: 80%: running totals are 22.40, 40.32, 54.66, 66.12, so **the 4th box**. 60%: the ceiling is 22.40 / 0.40 = $56, below $60, so **it never pays back**.
- Correct only if: gives the 4th box at 80%, and says never at 60% with the ceiling reason or a stated limit near $56 (tolerance: $55.50 to $56).
- Incorrect if: 3rd box (simple payback ignoring churn, 60 / 22.40 = 2.7); gives any finite box number at 60%, such as 7th or 8th; says never at 60% only because "churn is too high" with no ceiling or limit.

**Weak spot revealed.** `payback-ceiling-under-churn`. Lesson section: "Churn makes it longer, and can make it never". Adjusts for churn only partly, or misses that payback may never come.

## CAC-Q4

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional business adds 100 customers each month at a CAC of $60. Each customer brings $15 contribution a month. Assume no churn and no fixed costs, and that the first box ships in the month the customer is won. Starting from zero, what is the lowest point its cumulative cash reaches, and when? If it adds 200 customers a month instead, what is the lowest point?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: monthly net is 1,500n - 6,000: -4,500, -3,000, -1,500, 0, +1,500. Cumulative: -4,500, -7,500, **-9,000, -9,000**, -7,500. Lowest is **$9,000 down**, at the end of month 3, and held through month 4. At 200 a month it is **$18,000 down**, in the same months.
- Correct only if: gives $9,000 and $18,000, and places the low point in month 3 or months 3 to 4. Tolerance: exact. Naming only month 4 is also accepted.
- Incorrect if: $6,000 (one month of CAC); $4,500 (first month only); $7,500; a low point of $9,000 but growth not doubling it.

**Weak spot revealed.** `growth-consumes-cash`. Lesson section: "Good payback can still run out of cash". Sees payback per customer but not the cash needed while customers stack up.

## CAC-Q5

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional business spends $10,000 a month on ads and gains 200 customers. At $16,000 a month it would gain 260. A customer brings $90 of lifetime contribution. The founder says: "Average CAC would still be about $62, well below $90, so spend the extra." What do you advise, and why? Give the figures.

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: do not spend the extra. The extra 60 customers cost $6,000, so **marginal CAC is $100**, above $90. The extra spend brings 60 x 90 = $5,400 and **loses $600** (or $10 per extra customer).
- Correct only if: advises against the extra spend, gives $100 marginal CAC, and gives the $600 or $10 loss. Tolerance: exact.
- Incorrect if: advises spending because $61.54 is under $90; calculates $100 but still advises spending, unless the reason is a stated long-term benefit that outweighs the $600; $6,000 / 260 = $23.08.

**Weak spot revealed.** `marginal-vs-average-cac`. Lesson section: "The next customer costs more than the average". Decides on the average when the decision is about the next dollar.

## CAC-Q6

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional business spends $13,000 a month on acquisition. Reports credit paid channels with 250 of its 400 new monthly customers, giving a $52 CAC. It pauses all of that spend for a month, and new customers fall to 250. What is the real cost of each customer the spend causes, and what does this tell you about the $52?

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: the spend caused 400 - 250 = 150 customers, so incremental CAC is 13,000 / 150 = **$86.67**. Credit overstated the effect: 100 of the 250 credited customers would have come anyway, so $52 flatters the channels.
- Correct only if: gives $86.67 (tolerance: $86 to $87) and says the reports over-credit paid channels, or that some credited customers would have come anyway. A caveat that one month may understate lagged effects is welcome but not required.
- Incorrect if: $52 (reasserts the report); $130, $13,000 / 100 (divided by those who would have come anyway); $32.50 (blended).

**Weak spot revealed.** `credit-is-not-cause`. Lesson section: "Credit is not cause". Treats attribution reports as proof that spend caused customers.

## CAC-Q7

**Question.** Illustrative figures. A fictional box sells monthly at $40. Costs other than the payment fee are $16 a box, and the fee is 4% of money charged. CAC is $60. The business launches a $384 annual plan, paid up front for 12 boxes. The founder says: "Payback is now immediate, so this is better in every way." Ignoring churn, assess that claim. Give contribution per box, and the box in which CAC is recovered on contribution, under each plan.

**Marking notes.**
- Answer: only partly right. On cash, yes: $384 less a $15.36 fee is $368.64 at sign-up, more than the $60 CAC. On contribution, it is worse per box: monthly plan $22.40, recovered in the **3rd box** (2.68). Annual plan $32 - $16 - $1.28 = **$14.72**, recovered in the **5th box** (4.08). The customer is locked in for the year, which is a real benefit.
- Correct only if: separates cash payback from contribution payback, gives $22.40 and $14.72, and gives the 3rd and 5th boxes. Tolerance: exact on the per-box figures.
- Incorrect if: agrees payback is simply immediate; says annual is worse with no mention of the cash gain; $16.00 a box on the annual plan (32 - 16, left out the fee); a per-box figure that charges the fee twice.

**Weak spot revealed.** `cash-vs-contribution-payback`. Lesson section: "Prepayment changes cash, not unit economics". Treats earlier cash as better economics.
