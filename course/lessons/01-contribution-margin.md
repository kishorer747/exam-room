# Lesson 1: Contribution margin

> **Every figure in this lesson is illustrative and invented for teaching.** Tallow Street Beans is a fictional business. Any resemblance to a real one is coincidence.

Tallow Street Beans sells one monthly box of coffee for $30 (illustrative). This course follows it for three lessons.

## What one more box earns

Contribution margin (CM) is what one more sale leaves over after the costs that exist only because of that sale. It pays for everything else: first the fixed costs, then profit.

| Per box (illustrative) | $ |
|---|---|
| Price | 30.00 |
| Coffee | 9.00 |
| Packaging | 1.50 |
| Shipping | 5.00 |
| Payment fee, 3% of price | 0.90 |
| Outsourced support, charged per box | 0.60 |
| **Total variable cost** | **17.00** |
| **Contribution margin** | **13.00** |

The CM ratio is 13 / 30 = 43.3%.

## What counts as variable

Ask one question: if one more box ships, does this cost move? Coffee moves. Rent does not. Three cases catch people out:

- **Salaries with spare capacity are fixed.** A salaried agent who can take more customers costs the same whether or not this box ships. The cost jumps only when you need a second agent. This is a step cost.
- **Costs set as a percentage of price move when the price moves.** Refunds and payment fees are the usual examples.
- **Spending to win a new customer stays out of CM.** This course counts it in Lesson 2 as acquisition cost. If you count it in both places you charge for it twice.

## Contribution margin is not gross margin

Gross margin depends on which costs a business books as cost of goods. Tallow Street books only coffee and packaging, so its gross margin is (30 - 10.50) / 30 = 65%. But shipping, fees and support are real costs of each box. An extra dollar of sales adds about 43 cents towards fixed costs, not 65.

## Break-even

Fixed costs are $19,500 a month (illustrative: rent $4,000, salaries $14,500, software $1,000).

Break-even boxes = fixed costs / CM = 19,500 / 13 = **1,500 boxes**.

Divide by CM, never by price.

## Discounts cost more than they look

Take 20% off, so the price is $24. The payment fee falls with the price to $0.72, so variable cost is $16.82 and CM is **$7.18**.

- A 20% price cut removes (13 - 7.18) / 13 = 44.8% of the contribution from each box.
- To earn the same total contribution you need 13 / 7.18 = 1.81 times the boxes, which is **81% more**. At the break-even volume, that means about 2,716 boxes rather than 1,500.

## Ratio or dollars: ask what is scarce

Suppose a small box earns $8 on a $20 price (40%) and a large box earns $13.50 on a $45 price (30%) (illustrative). Which is better depends on what limits you.

- If the limit is revenue you can win for a set ad budget, the ratio decides. On $900 of sales, small earns $360 and large earns $270.
- If the limit is the number of customers you can serve, dollars per customer decide, and large wins.

## The full-cost trap

At 1,500 boxes, fixed costs spread out to $13 a box, so an accountant may quote a "full cost" of $30. That number is only true at exactly that volume, and it does not change what one more box costs.

A cafe offers $22 a box for 200 boxes (illustrative). It collects them and pays by invoice, so only coffee and packaging are incurred. That is $10.50 of variable cost, which adds $11.50 a box and $2,300 in total. The "$8 loss per box" against full cost is not real, as long as there is spare capacity and the cafe's price does not pull down what regular subscribers pay.

## Remember

1. CM counts only costs that move with one more sale.
2. Divide fixed costs by CM to find break-even.
3. Recalculate percentage costs whenever the price changes.
4. Compare options on whichever resource is scarce.
